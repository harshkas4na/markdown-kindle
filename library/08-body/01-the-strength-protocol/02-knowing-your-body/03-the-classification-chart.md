# The Classification Chart

This is the chart you came for. It runs in three passes: your frame, your current state, and the instruction that falls out of the combination.

Pass 2 is the one that decides what you do next week. Pass 1 mostly decides your expectations and your patience.

## Pass 1 — Frame

Divide your wrist circumference by your height. Both in centimetres. Do the same for your ankle.

| Frame | Wrist ÷ height | Ankle ÷ height |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrow** | below 0.1045 | below 0.1296 |
| **Medium** | 0.1045 to 0.115 | 0.1296 to 0.142 |
| **Wide** | above 0.115 | above 0.142 |

The narrow cutoffs come from Casey Butt, a drug-tested bodybuilder who analysed the measurements of top-level natural competitors and used them to define what he called a hardgainer frame. Here are the same thresholds pre-computed, so you don't have to:

| Height | Narrow wrist below | Wide wrist above | Narrow ankle below | Wide ankle above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 160 cm | 16.7 cm | 18.4 cm | 20.7 cm | 22.7 cm |
| 165 cm | 17.2 cm | 19.0 cm | 21.4 cm | 23.4 cm |
| 170 cm | 17.8 cm | 19.6 cm | 22.0 cm | 24.1 cm |
| 175 cm | 18.3 cm | 20.1 cm | 22.7 cm | 24.9 cm |
| 180 cm | 18.8 cm | 20.7 cm | 23.3 cm | 25.6 cm |
| 185 cm | 19.3 cm | 21.3 cm | 24.0 cm | 26.3 cm |
| 190 cm | 19.9 cm | 21.9 cm | 24.6 cm | 27.0 cm |

**What your frame means.** A wider frame supports more muscle at the same height and tends to reach a heavier eventual ceiling. A narrower frame reaches a lower absolute ceiling and, at the same body weight, will look leaner and more defined — the same 75 kg looks very different draped over a narrow skeleton than a wide one.

**What your frame does not mean.** It does not tell you to train differently. It does not tell you to eat differently. A narrow-framed person and a wide-framed person on the same good program both get stronger and both build muscle; they just have different asymptotes and reach visible results on different timelines. If you take one thing from Pass 1, take this: a narrow frame is a reason to be patient and to stop comparing yourself to a specific stranger on the internet. It is not a reason to do anything different on Monday.

Sensitivity check: a 1 cm difference in wrist circumference moves the predicted maximum lean mass by roughly 2–3 kg. So measure carefully, and don't over-read a borderline result.

## Pass 2 — Current state

This is the pass that produces an instruction. Two inputs: your body fat estimate from the last chapter, and your training age.

**Training age** is not how long you've had a gym membership. It's how long you have trained *with progressive overload, consistently, at least twice a week.*

| Training age | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Untrained** | Never trained, or under 6 months of consistent lifting |
| **Returning** | Previously trained for 1+ years, then off for 6+ months |
| **Novice** | 6–18 months consistent, still adding weight to the bar most weeks |
| **Intermediate** | 18 months to ~4 years, progress now measured monthly not weekly |
| **Advanced** | 4+ years, progress measured over a year, near your ceiling |

Now find your row:

| # | State | Men BF% | Women BF% | Training age | **Instruction** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Untrained & lean | under 13% | under 23% | Untrained | **Lean bulk.** You have the rarest opportunity in this table — a slight surplus plus newbie gains. Do not waste it cutting. |
| 2 | Untrained & average | 13–19% | 23–30% | Untrained | **Recomp at maintenance.** Eat at roughly maintenance, high protein, train hard. You will gain muscle and lose fat simultaneously. This works for you and will never work this well again. |
| 3 | Untrained & high | 20%+ | 31%+ | Untrained | **Moderate cut.** Small deficit, high protein, full training. You will still build muscle in a deficit — the stored energy and untrained state make it possible. Get to roughly 15% / 26% first. |
| 4 | Returning, any BF | any | any | Returning | **Recomp, aggressively.** Muscle memory is real and rapid. Do not treat yourself as a beginner in the weight room, but do restart at ~70% of your old loads to let connective tissue catch up. |
| 5 | Trained & lean | under 12% | under 22% | Novice+ | **Lean bulk.** You're past the free lunch. Slow surplus, patient timeline. |
| 6 | Trained & average | 12–17% | 22–28% | Novice+ | **Pick one and commit for 12+ weeks.** Bulk if size is the goal, cut if leanness is. The failure mode here is oscillating every three weeks and doing neither. |
| 7 | Trained & high | 18%+ | 29%+ | Novice+ | **Cut.** Bulking from here buys mostly fat. Get to 12–14% / 22–24%, then bulk from a better starting point. |

## Pass 3 — Read your instruction back to yourself

Write it down as one sentence with a date and a number. Not "I'm going to get in shape." Something like:

> *From 29 July, I am doing a lean bulk. Target rate: +0.3 kg per week. I re-evaluate on 21 October.*

The date matters as much as the instruction. The single most common pattern in stalled progress is not choosing the wrong direction — it's changing direction before the current one had time to produce a measurable result. Twelve weeks is the minimum honest evaluation window. Anything shorter is noise.

## The three traps in this chart

**Trap 1: state 3 people who bulk.** If you're above 20% body fat and untrained, the "you need to bulk to build muscle" advice does not apply to you — you already have a large energy surplus attached to your midsection, and your untrained muscles will grow on it. Bulking here just makes the eventual cut longer and more miserable.

**Trap 2: state 1 people who cut.** If you're a lean untrained person who thinks they need to "lose the last bit first," you are about to throw away the highest-return training year of your life. There is nothing to lose. Eat.

**Trap 3: everyone in state 6, forever.** Most people who have trained for a while and are neither lean nor fat spend years in this state, because every direction feels like a sacrifice. Bulking means accepting looking softer for a few months. Cutting means accepting being weaker and hungrier. Refusing both means staying exactly where you are, which is the only option that guarantees no progress. Pick. Commit for twelve weeks. You can always pick the other one afterward.

## Re-run this chart every twelve weeks

Your frame won't change. Your state will. The instruction that's right for you today is wrong for you in a year, and re-running the chart is how you notice.
