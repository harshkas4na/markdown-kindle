# Your Response Profile

This is the chapter that replaces "what body type am I." You cannot fill it in today. You fill it in after eight to twelve weeks of training, from your logbook — and once you have, you'll know more about how your body works than any somatotype chart could ever have told you.

Copy this into your notes now. Answer it in three months.

## The six axes

### 1. Appetite

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Hitting a surplus feels like a chore. You forget meals. You feel full on 2,200 kcal. |
| **Medium** | Eating more is mildly effortful but fine. |
| **High** | You are hungry constantly. Deficits are miserable. Maintenance takes attention. |

*What it changes:* everything about how you approach nutrition. Low appetite means calorie-dense foods, liquid calories, eating on a schedule rather than on hunger. High appetite means volume foods, high fibre, high protein, and structural rules rather than willpower. This one variable explains most of what people mistake for "fast metabolism."

### 2. NEAT and fidget level

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Low** | You're still. Desk work, few steps, sit through calls. |
| **High** | You pace, gesture, can't sit for long, high daily step count without trying. |

*What it changes:* your actual maintenance calories, by several hundred a day. This is largely not under conscious control and it varies enormously between people. It's the second half of the "fast metabolism" explanation, and it's why your friend's calorie target will not be yours.

### 3. Recovery between sessions

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Fast** | Sore for a day at most. Ready to train the same muscle in 48 hours. |
| **Slow** | Sore 3+ days. Performance still down on the second session of the week. |

*What it changes:* frequency and volume. Fast recovery earns you higher-frequency splits and more sets. Slow recovery means fewer sets per session, more rest days, and paying much closer attention to sleep. Note that soreness is a poor proxy on its own — *performance* is the real signal. Are you weaker on Thursday than you were on Monday?

### 4. Volume tolerance

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Low** | More than ~10 hard sets per muscle per week and everything gets worse. |
| **High** | 20+ sets per muscle per week and you keep progressing. |

*What it changes:* the single biggest program variable. And it's genuinely individual — the research supports a broad 10–20 sets per muscle per week range precisely because the response varies so much within it. Finding your number is Experiment 1 in Part 6.

### 5. Strength versus size response

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Strength-dominant** | Numbers on the bar climb fast, mirror changes slowly. |
| **Size-dominant** | You visibly fill out but the bar moves slowly. |
| **Balanced** | Both track together. |

*What it changes:* which rep ranges you emphasise, and which metric you should use to judge whether a block worked. A strength-dominant person judging a hypertrophy block by the mirror will wrongly conclude it failed.

### 6. Fat distribution

| Reading | What you'd observe |
|---|---|
| **Abdominal first** | Weight lands on the midsection first, comes off there last. |
| **Hips and thighs first** | Weight lands lower body first. |
| **Even** | Distributed. |

*What it changes:* nothing you can control — you cannot spot-reduce, and any product that claims otherwise is lying. What it does change is *how you read your own progress*. If you store abdominally, your waist measurement is a high-sensitivity instrument and you should trust it. If you don't, waist will move less and you'll need photos and the scale to see the same signal.

## Why this beats a body type

Look at what these six axes cross-cut. A person could plausibly be low-appetite, high-NEAT, fast-recovering, high-volume-tolerant, strength-dominant and abdominal-storing. There is no three-letter word for that person. There are 3 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 216 combinations here and the popular framework offers three.

More importantly: every one of these readings comes with a *specific* change to make. "You're an ectomorph" comes with nothing but a vibe. "Your appetite is low and your NEAT is high, so your true maintenance is above what the calculator said and you need to make eating structural rather than intuitive" comes with a grocery list.

## The honest caveat

You will be tempted to fill this in today, from memory and self-image. Don't. Almost everyone predicts their own volume tolerance wrong, and about half of people who describe themselves as having a "fast metabolism" are simply eating less than they think — the most consistent finding in the entire self-report literature on skinny people who can't gain weight is that when they finally track intake accurately, it's far below what they estimated.

Fill it in from the log. The log is not sentimental about you.
