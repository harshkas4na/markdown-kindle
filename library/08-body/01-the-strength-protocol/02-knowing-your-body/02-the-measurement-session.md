# The Measurement Session

Set aside forty minutes, once. You need a cloth tape measure, a scale, a phone camera, and a room where you won't be interrupted. Do this before you read Part 3.

Everything that follows in this book branches off these numbers. Skipping this chapter and reading on is the equivalent of running a program with no logbook — you'll be doing something, but you won't be able to tell whether it worked.

## Rules for measuring

- **Morning, fasted, after the bathroom, before drinking.** This isn't fussiness; a full stomach and a litre of water move the scale by 1–2 kg and the waist by 2 cm, which is the entire size of the signal you're trying to detect later.
- **Tape snug, not tight.** It should sit flat against skin without denting it. Same tension every time.
- **Same spot, every time.** Where exactly you measure the waist matters less than measuring the *same* place in twelve weeks. Pick a landmark — navel, or the narrowest point — and write down which you chose.
- **Measure twice, average.** If the two readings differ by more than 0.5 cm, do a third.

## Sheet A — the fixed numbers

These barely change. You take them once and they inform everything about your realistic ceiling.

| Measurement | How to take it | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| **Height** (cm) | Barefoot, heels to wall, morning | |
| **Wrist** (cm) | Narrowest point, *below* the wrist bone toward the hand, non-dominant arm | |
| **Ankle** (cm) | Narrowest point, just above the ankle bones | |
| **Knee** (cm) | Across the kneecap, leg straight and relaxed | |
| **Shoulder-to-navel vs navel-to-floor** | Rough torso/leg proportion — long femurs change which lifts suit you | |

Wrist and ankle are the important two. They're chosen because almost no soft tissue covers them, so they're a clean proxy for skeletal size and they do not change with training. Fat gain doesn't move your wrist. Neither does five years of squatting.

## Sheet B — the current numbers

These are the ones you re-take every four weeks. Circle the date.

| Measurement | How to take it | Yours |
|---|---|---|
| **Bodyweight** (kg) | Morning, fasted, post-bathroom | |
| **Neck** (cm) | Just below the larynx, tape sloping slightly down at the front | |
| **Waist** (cm) | At the navel, relaxed — do not suck in, do not push out | |
| **Hips** (cm) | Widest point of the glutes | |
| **Chest** (cm) | At nipple line, arms down, at the end of a normal exhale | |
| **Upper arm** (cm) | Midway between shoulder and elbow, relaxed and straight | |
| **Thigh** (cm) | Midway between hip crease and knee | |
| **Calf** (cm) | Widest point, standing, weight evenly on both feet | |

## Sheet C — the photographs

Take four: front, back, both sides. Same room, same light, same time of day, phone at chest height on a stable surface, minimal clothing, relaxed posture — not flexed, not sucked in. Then put them somewhere you will not look at them for twelve weeks.

This feels pointless and it is the single most valuable thing on this page. The scale is noisy and the mirror lies in both directions — it flatters you on good days and it's brutal on bad ones, and neither reading is information. Two photographs twelve weeks apart is information. Almost every long-term progress report you'll read from people who succeeded mentions the photos, and almost every one from people who stalled mentions that they didn't take any.

## Estimating body fat

You need this number, roughly, because Chapter 3 branches on it. You do not need it precisely, and nothing available to you is precise.

**The tape method (US Navy formula).** Free, repeatable, and accurate to within about 3–4 percentage points. All measurements in centimetres.

For men:

```
BF% = 495 / (1.0324 - 0.19077 × log10(waist - neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) - 450
```

For women:

```
BF% = 495 / (1.29579 - 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip - neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) - 450
```

**The visual method.** Cross-check the tape number against this. If the two disagree wildly, trust the visual one — the formula degrades badly at the extremes and on very muscular people.

| Men | What you see |
|---|---|
| 6–9% | Full abs visible relaxed, vascularity across abdomen, striations on shoulders |
| 10–12% | Abs visible relaxed in good light, defined obliques, veins on arms |
| 13–15% | Upper abs visible, waist clearly narrower than chest, some softness at the navel |
| 16–19% | No visible abs standing relaxed, mild overhang when seated, face still defined |
| 20–24% | Rounded midsection, chest softening, waist near or over chest measurement |
| 25%+ | Waist clearly the widest point of the torso |

| Women | What you see |
|---|---|
| 14–17% | Abs visible, striated shoulders — very low, hard to maintain, often disrupts menstrual function |
| 18–22% | Ab outline visible, defined arms and legs, athletic |
| 23–27% | Firm but no visible abs, curved hips, healthy athletic default |
| 28–32% | Softer midsection and hips, no muscle separation visible |
| 33%+ | Rounded throughout, midsection prominent |

**What not to use.** Bathroom scales with "body fat" readouts are hydration meters with a marketing department; they can swing 5% based on how much water you drank. Gym calliper tests are only as good as the person pinching, which is usually not very. DEXA is genuinely good, costs money, and is still ±2% between machines. None of this matters much — you need a bucket, not a decimal place.

## The one number to actually watch

Long term, the metric with the best signal-to-noise ratio is not weight and it is not body fat percentage. It's **waist circumference plotted against bodyweight**.

Weight going up with waist flat means you are gaining muscle. Weight going up with waist going up proportionally means you are mostly gaining fat. Weight flat with waist down means recomposition is happening and the scale is hiding it — which is the exact scenario where people quit, convinced nothing is working, three weeks before it becomes visible.

Two numbers, once a week, same conditions. That's the whole tracking system.
