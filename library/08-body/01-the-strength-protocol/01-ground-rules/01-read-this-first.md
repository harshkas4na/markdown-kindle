# Read This First

This book exists because of a specific failure mode. You walk into a gym, you want to know what to do, and the internet hands you ten thousand answers that all contradict each other and all sound confident. So you pick one at random, run it for five weeks, see nothing, and conclude that your body is the problem.

Your body is not the problem. The problem is that you were given a *recipe* when what you needed was an *instrument*.

## What this book actually gives you

Three things, in this order.

**A way to type yourself that isn't astrology.** You asked for a chart to find out what body type you are. You will get one — Part 2 is nothing but charts. But it will not be the ectomorph/mesomorph/endomorph chart you've seen on every fitness site, because that chart is a 1940s theory that failed and it will actively mislead you. What you get instead is a set of measurements you take with a tape measure and a scale, which sort you into a *starting state* — and a starting state tells you what to do next week, which is the only thing a classification is for.

**A default program and a default diet.** Not because they are optimal for you — nobody, including me, knows what is optimal for you yet — but because you need a baseline to run the experiment against. A mediocre program run for six months beats a perfect program run for three weeks, and this is not a motivational cliché, it is the single most replicated finding in this entire field.

**A loop for improving on both.** Part 6 is the point of the book. Everything before it is the starting position; the loop is how you move from a starting position to something that is actually yours. You run a block, you measure, you change exactly one thing, you run again.

## What it does not give you

No supplements chapter beyond a paragraph, because the honest supplements chapter is four items long. No "shocking your muscles." No advice that requires you to buy anything except food. No promises about a timeline, because timelines are the thing every dishonest fitness product sells and every honest one refuses to.

## How to read it

Part 2 is homework, not reading. Do not read past it until you have taken the measurements. The entire rest of the book branches off the numbers you get there, and reading the training and eating chapters without knowing whether you should be gaining or losing weight is how people end up doing both badly at once for a year.

After that: Parts 3, 4, and 5 are reference. Read them once, then come back to the specific chapter when the loop tells you to change something. Part 7 is what everyone else found out the hard way, and it's the part worth re-reading when you're two months in and bored.

## The one honest warning

Every number in this book is a population average, and you are one person. A meta-analysis that says 1.6 g of protein per kg is the breakpoint is describing where the *average* response curve flattens across 1,863 people. It is not describing you. It is describing the best place for you to *start guessing*.

That gap — between what the research knows about people and what you need to know about yourself — is the entire reason Part 6 exists. Treat the numbers here as priors, not as answers. Your job over the next year is to update them.
