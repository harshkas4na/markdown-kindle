# The Tracking System

The whole system, in the smallest form that still works. Anything more elaborate than this gets abandoned; anything less doesn't produce enough signal to steer by.

## Daily — 30 seconds

| What | When |
|---|---|
| **Bodyweight** | Morning, fasted, after the bathroom, before drinking |

That's it, on non-training days. One number.

## Every training session — 2 minutes

| What | Format |
|---|---|
| **Date and workout** | A / B, or Upper A, etc. |
| **Each exercise: weight × reps per set** | `Squat 80kg — 5/5/5` |
| **RIR on the last set** (optional) | `RIR 2` |
| **One-line note** (optional) | "Left shoulder tight" / "slept 5h" |

Paper notebook, notes app, or a lifting app — it genuinely doesn't matter which. What matters is that you can look at last week's entry *before* this week's set, because that's what makes progression a decision instead of a guess.

## Weekly — 5 minutes, same day each week

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| **Average of the last 7 daily weights** | The only bodyweight number worth acting on |
| **Waist measurement** | Morning, relaxed, same landmark |
| **Sessions completed / sessions planned** | Adherence, the layer-1 variable |
| **Average sleep hours** | The variable you'll otherwise lie to yourself about |

Then one question: **is the weekly average moving at my target rate?** Bulking should be +0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week; cutting −0.5–0.75%; recomping flat with the waist falling. If it's off target for two consecutive weeks, adjust by 200 kcal. Not sooner.

## Every four weeks — 20 minutes

- **Full measurement sheet** — the Sheet B set from Part 2, chapter 2.
- **Photos** — same four angles, same room, same light, same time of day.
- **Body fat estimate** — the Navy tape formula, plus the visual cross-check.
- **Log review** — for each main lift, is the weight or the reps higher than four weeks ago? Write down which lifts are progressing and which aren't.

That last item is the review most people skip and it's the one that catches problems early. A lift that hasn't moved in four weeks is information; a lift that hasn't moved in four *months* is a wasted quarter.

## Every twelve weeks — the block review

1. **Close the experiment card.** Fill in the result, the confounders, and the verdict.
2. **Compare photos** — week 1 versus week 12, side by side. This is the moment the tracking pays for itself, and it's the reason the week-1 photos exist.
3. **Re-run the Pass 2 classification chart.** Your state has probably changed. The instruction may have too.
4. **Update your Response Profile** with anything the block taught you.
5. **Write the next block's card** before you start it.

## What not to track

Deliberately excluded, because tracking costs attention and attention is finite:

- **Daily calories, forever.** Track for four weeks to calibrate, then re-calibrate when progress stalls. Permanent tracking burns people out and the added precision isn't worth it.
- **Every micronutrient.** Eat vegetables.
- **Heart rate variability, sleep scores, readiness scores.** These are noisy, they correlate poorly with what you actually want to know, and they mostly generate anxiety on days you would otherwise have trained fine.
- **Water weight, day-to-day fluctuations, single-day scale readings.** Noise. Looking at them daily and reacting is worse than not measuring.

## The one-page version

If you do nothing else in this book, do these three things:

1. **Log every set.**
2. **Weigh yourself daily, act on the weekly average.**
3. **Photograph yourself every twelve weeks.**

Those three take about four minutes a week combined. They convert training from a thing you do into a thing you can steer, and steering is the entire difference between someone who's been going to the gym for three years and someone who's been *training* for three years. The gap between those two people is enormous and it is almost entirely a measurement gap.
