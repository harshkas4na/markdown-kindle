# Your First Twelve Weeks

The whole book, compressed into a schedule. Follow it literally.

## Week 0 — Setup

- [ ] Take Sheet A measurements — height, wrist, ankle. Compute your frame.
- [ ] Take Sheet B measurements — weight, neck, waist, hips, chest, arm, thigh, calf.
- [ ] Take the four photographs. Put them somewhere you won't look until week 12.
- [ ] Estimate body fat, tape formula plus visual cross-check.
- [ ] Run the Pass 2 chart. Write your instruction as one sentence with a date.
- [ ] Calculate TDEE. Write down your calorie and protein targets.
- [ ] Choose your tier — almost certainly Tier 1 — and write the days in your calendar.
- [ ] Set up the log. Notebook or app, decided today.
- [ ] Fill in the top half of Experiment Card #1. Hypothesis: *the baseline program at my prescribed calories will produce measurable strength gain and the target weight trend.*

## Weeks 1–2 — Learn the movements

**Training.** Run the program with weights that feel easy. Deliberately easy. Film your squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press and row from the side. Watch them. Fix the obvious faults against the table in Part 3, chapter 4.

**Eating.** Track everything, weighed, at your calculated maintenance. Don't try to bulk or cut yet — you're calibrating the instrument.

**The temptation you must resist:** loading up because it feels too light. Two weeks. You have years ahead of you.

## Weeks 3–4 — Load

**Training.** Start adding weight every session. This is the fastest progress you will ever experience — enjoy it, and don't be misled by it. It slows down and that's normal.

**Eating.** Compare week 1's average weight to week 2's, adjust to find true maintenance, then shift to your Pass 2 target — surplus, deficit, or maintenance.

**End of week 4:** full measurement sheet, photos, log review.

## Weeks 5–8 — The hard part

This is the window where roughly half of people quit. Weeks six to ten are the statistical danger zone: the novelty has worn off, the initial rapid gains have slowed, and nothing visible has happened yet.

Three things get you through it.

**Know that the timeline is normal.** Meaningful visible change takes about three months for you to notice and closer to a year for other people to comment. Nothing is wrong. You are exactly on schedule.

**Watch the log, not the mirror.** Your squat has probably gone up 20 kg since week 1. That is the result. The mirror is lagging behind by design.

**Lower the bar rather than miss.** On a bad week, do the three main lifts and go home. A twenty-minute session preserves the habit; a skipped one starts a chain. The people who make it are not the ones who never had bad weeks — they're the ones whose bad weeks produced short sessions instead of missed ones.

Expect a plateau on at least one lift, probably the overhead press. Deload it 10% and climb back. That's the system working, not failing.

## Weeks 9–12 — Consolidate

**Training.** Linear progression is slowing on the big lifts. Some are still climbing, some have moved to double progression. This is exactly right.

**Take a deload week around week 9 or 10** if you haven't already — reduced load or reduced volume, one week. You will come back stronger, and it will feel like it came from nowhere.

**Eating.** Your maintenance has drifted. Re-check against the weekly averages and adjust.

## Week 12 — The review

Set aside an hour.

1. Full measurements. New photos.
2. **Put week 0 and week 12 photos side by side.** This is the payoff. Whatever the scale says, this comparison is what tells you the truth.
3. Close Experiment Card #1 — actual numbers, confounders, verdict.
4. Re-run the Pass 2 chart with your new numbers.
5. Update the Response Profile — what did you learn about your appetite, recovery, volume tolerance?
6. Write Card #2. One variable.

## What twelve weeks should have produced

Honest expectations, for an untrained person who did this properly:

| Measure | Realistic result |
|---|---|
| Squat | +25 to +40 kg from a light start |
| Bench | +15 to +25 kg |
| Deadlift | +30 to +50 kg |
| Overhead press | +10 to +15 kg |
| Muscle gained | 2–3 kg |
| Visible change | Noticeable to you in photos; probably not commented on by others yet |
| Habit | The gym is now a default rather than a decision |

That last row is the one that matters most and it's the one nobody puts in a table. Research on exercise adherence suggests it takes roughly eight to twelve weeks of consistent attendance before the behaviour starts to feel automatic. If you get to week twelve, you have crossed the line that most people never cross — and everything after it is easier than everything before it.

## If you missed weeks

Almost everyone does. Do not restart the clock and do not compensate with extra sessions.

Count what you actually did. If you hit 30 of 36 planned sessions, that's 83% adherence and it's genuinely good. If you hit 15, the problem isn't the program and no program change will fix it — the problem is scheduling, and it needs a scheduling solution: fewer days, a closer gym, a fixed time, a training partner, a lower bar for what counts as a session.

Diagnose the actual constraint. Then run week 0 again, honestly, with a plan that fits the life you have rather than the one you were imagining in December.
