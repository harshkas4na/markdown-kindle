# Form, Injury, and the Long Game

Nothing in this book matters if you're injured. An injury doesn't just cost you the weeks you're hurt — it costs the momentum, the habit, and often the year, because a large fraction of people who get hurt in month four never come back at all.

## The four rules that prevent most gym injuries

**1. Learn the lift before you load the lift.** Spend the first two weeks with an empty bar or very light weight, filming yourself from the side, on every compound. Two weeks feels like an eternity when you're motivated. It's 1.5% of your first year.

**2. Never let ego set the weight.** The weight is set by your rep target at your RIR target. If the last rep of a set of five looked like a rescue attempt, that weight was wrong regardless of whether it went up.

**3. Sharp pain stops the set. Immediately.** Distinguish two sensations. Muscular burn, difficulty, and next-day soreness are training. A sharp, localised, joint-line pain — especially one that appears at the same point in the range every rep — is a warning. Working through the second kind is the most common route to a six-month problem.

**4. Progress the loading rate, not just the load.** Connective tissue — tendons, ligaments — adapts more slowly than muscle. This is exactly why returning lifters get hurt: the muscle remembers the old weights within weeks, the tendons don't. Restart at ~70% and take a month to climb back.

## The five lifts that hurt people, and why

| Lift | The failure | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Deadlift** | Lower back rounds under load, hips shoot up first | Brace hard before the pull, lats tight, drag the bar up the shins. Reduce weight until the back stays neutral on every rep. |
| **Bench press** | Shoulders roll forward, elbows flare to 90° | Retract and depress the shoulder blades, elbows ~45–60° from torso, feet planted, slight arch. |
| **Squat** | Knees collapse inward, or the torso pitches forward as the hips rise faster than the shoulders | Push knees out over the toes, brace the whole trunk, drive hips and chest at the same rate. Front squats expose this fault instantly. |
| **Overhead press** | Excessive lumbar arch to get under the bar | Squeeze glutes, ribs down, brace abs. If you can't press it without leaning back, it's too heavy. |
| **Barbell row** | Torso rises through the set until it's a shrug | Fix the torso angle and hold it. If it drifts, drop the weight — chest-supported rows remove the fault entirely. |

## Warm-up: what's worth doing

Five to ten minutes, and it's not stretching.

- **Three to five minutes of general movement.** Bike, rower, brisk walk. Enough to raise your temperature slightly.
- **Ramp-up sets on the first compound.** Empty bar × 10, then 3–5 reps at roughly 40%, 60%, 80% of your working weight. This is the warm-up that actually matters.
- **Skip long static stretching before lifting.** Held stretches before heavy work transiently reduce force output. Save them for after, or a separate session.

## Mobility, honestly

You probably need less than the internet implies and more than you're doing. Two specific things pay off for most people who sit at a desk:

- **Ankle dorsiflexion** — if your heels lift in a squat or you can't stay upright, the ankle is usually the culprit before the hips. Knee-to-wall stretch, daily, two minutes.
- **Thoracic extension** — if you can't get the bar overhead without arching your lower back, your upper back is stiff. Foam roller extensions, daily, two minutes.

Beyond those two, the best mobility work is full-range-of-motion lifting. A deep squat trains the range it uses.

## Small things that add up over years

- **Face pulls or band pull-aparts, 2–3 sets, most sessions.** The cheapest shoulder insurance available. Costs four minutes.
- **Train your calves and your neck.** Nobody does, and both are load-bearing for everything else.
- **Don't wear a belt for everything.** For heavy sets near your limit, fine. For everything, it teaches you not to brace.
- **Straps are not cheating.** If grip is the limiting factor on your rows and heavy pulls, your back isn't getting trained. Use straps for the working sets, train grip separately if you want it.
- **Shoes matter for squatting and deadlifting.** Anything with a compressible foam sole makes you less stable. Flat shoes or barefoot for pulls; flat or a heeled lifting shoe for squats.

## The long game

The people who end up looking like they lift are not the people who trained hardest in any given month. They are the people who were still training in year six. Every decision in this chapter is subordinate to that: take the weight that lets you train next week, not the weight that proves something today.

> A recurring line in long-term progress reports, in almost identical words from very different people: *the only workouts that mattered were the ones I did on the days I didn't want to.* Not because grinding is virtuous — because those are the days that decide whether this is a habit or a phase.
