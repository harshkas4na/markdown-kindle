# Where The Money Actually Is

Read this chapter even if you skip everything else, because it's about the largest number in your record and the one you're most likely to leave on the table.

## The arithmetic

**November 2024, International Reactive Hackathon.** Two submissions, 1st and 2nd place, $2,000 each. Four thousand dollars.

Then: a **$9,000 grant**. Four press features across Chainwire, CryptoSlate, Blockchain Magazine and BlockTelegraph. REACTOR became ReacDEFI, which went to production and guards real Aave positions. And the whole chain ended in the Reactive Network job — intern in April, Solidity and DApp Developer by July.

**The prize was the smallest part of that outcome by an order of magnitude.** Everything else came afterwards, from work you did after the judging ended.

That's why compounding return is weighted 25% in the scorer — the second-heaviest axis, above cost and trust. And it's why `/downstream` exists as its own step rather than a line in a post-mortem, because in the version where it's a line in a post-mortem it doesn't happen.

## Run it on losses

This is the part that's easy to get wrong. Two of your most valuable career artifacts did not come from winning anything:

**The Walrus SDK** went to npm after ETHIndia. Still there, still earning.

**`leverage-loop`** was merged into Reactive Network's official `reactive-smart-contract-demos` repo. Someone else's protocol adopted your contracts as the reference material their developers learn from. That's arguably the strongest single credential in `facts.yaml`, and no prize was attached to it.

**A loss with a good artifact routinely outperforms a win without one.** So `/downstream` runs after every submission regardless of result.

## The seven channels, in order of payoff

**1. Sponsor and judge follow-up — within 48 hours.** The window is short and almost nobody uses it. While the event is fresh, the devrel still remembers your project. The move is a short note: what you built, the live link, one specific thing you learned about their tech, and **one honest piece of feedback about their developer experience**. The feedback is the hook — it reads as a peer rather than a supplicant, and devrels are measured on DX. This is the only channel that decays fast; everything else can wait a week.

**2. The grant path.** Most protocols running a hackathon also run a grants program, and a working submission is already most of a grant application. The honest test is whether the project serves *other developers* in that ecosystem and would keep existing after the event. That's the shape that got funded. This is the single highest-EV action anywhere in the pipeline.

**3. Upstream contribution.** Would any component work as a PR to the sponsor's repo, their demos directory, their docs, their SDK? Cheap, high status, permanent, and it survives every future job search. `leverage-loop` is the proof.

**4. Ship the artifact properly.** npm, a live URL, a template repo, a Claude skill. Then add it to `prior-art.md` **with what it re-aims at**, which is what makes the next hackathon cheaper. This is how the system compounds into itself.

**5. Content.** Your distribution already exists — roughly a thousand followers, thirteen-plus Medium deep-dives, camera-facing video, and the Remotion chalkboard tooling you built. Learning-log posts are consistently your highest-engagement content, and the build just produced a genuine technical lesson. Tag the sponsor; it's also how devrels find you.

**6. Press — only with a real hook.** The four REACTOR features happened because there was a story: first production DApp on the network, plus a grant. Crypto press covers funded, first-of-kind, and unusual-number stories. No hook, no pitch — a pitch without a story burns the contact.

**7. The job-hunt handoff.** Cross-check sponsors and judges against `../job-ops/applications/targets.md`. A sponsor who liked your submission is the warmest possible intro, and JobOps has a whole pipeline for exactly that — hand it over rather than rebuilding it here. Add the project to `stories.md` if it produced a good interview answer.

## Measured in months

Every action gets logged in `ledger.md` under the event's Downstream line, with a **3-month recheck** scheduled. The Reactive chain took most of a year to fully pay out, and an event that looks like a zero at close can look very different two quarters later.

Which is the real argument for logging losses honestly. You cannot see a compounding return you never recorded the start of.

## The thing to actually remember

You are good at the part everyone thinks is the hard part. You can build whatever you want to build — that's been the bio since 2023 and it's true now.

The parts that are actually hard are the ones this system holds for you: **picking the right room**, which chapter 1 says means the thin track rather than the famous one; **finishing**, which means the last day is deploy and README and video, not one more feature; and **collecting**, which means the forty-eight hours after judging where the grant and the sponsor relationship and the upstream PR are still available and nobody bothers.

Build and win. The tracking is handled.
