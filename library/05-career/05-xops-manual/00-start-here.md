# Start Here

You already know how to write like yourself. What you don't have, when you're mid-scroll on X and someone posts something worth answering, is time to notice it, remember who they are to you, and type something that sounds like you instead of like a reply-guy. X-Ops is that gap, compiled into software. It lives at `PROJECTS/Me/x-ops`, and this manual is about operating it.

## The one idea underneath everything

Every other pipeline you run — JobOps, HackOps, learn-ops — is a batch system. Something scouts overnight, a brief lands in the morning, you act on the brief. X-Ops is shaped differently on purpose, and the reason is worth sitting with: **a reply is not a scheduled decision.** It fires the instant you're looking at the post, not twelve hours later when a brief gets read.

That has a sharper consequence than it sounds like. The single most valuable thing this system could ever learn is **which posts you scrolled past without replying** — and a morning brief, sitting in a terminal, can never see that. If you read a list in one tab and reply in another, nothing observes what you chose, and the tool learns nothing, forever. So the surface has to be the timeline itself. That's the whole reason this is a Chrome extension and not another script that dumps a file into an inbox.

## What the system owns vs what you own

The system owns the mechanical work: watching the timeline, scoring every post that streams past, holding a draft ready the instant you want one, remembering which of your 4,696 lifetime replies went to which of 122 people, and writing down every choice you make so it never has to be re-explained. It also owns the parts you'd never do by hand — reading a stranger's whole thread before drafting a check-my-understanding question, or noticing a circle member has gone quiet for two weeks.

You own three things, and they matter in this order:

1. **The send.** Every draft this system produces stops in a composer, unsent, forever. Nothing here posts, replies, likes, follows, or DMs on its own — not once, not as a convenience feature waiting to be turned on. Chapter 7 explains why that isn't caution, it's the entire mechanism.
2. **The voice.** The corpus tells the system your *register* — lowercase, informal, no hype words. It does not get to decide what you actually say, and Chapter 1 is built entirely around a correction you gave the system directly on this point.
3. **The judgment about people.** Who's in your circle, who you want to be closer to a year from now, which experiment is worth running on your actual follower list. Chapter 4 is where that judgment gets infrastructure.

## How to read this

Chapter 1 is the theory that everything else descends from — read it first, because it corrects something the first build of this system got wrong and every file since then is written around the fix. Chapter 2 covers the two logs that make the rest of the system honest. Chapter 3 walks one reply end to end, from a post appearing on your timeline to a send landing in the decision log. Chapter 4 is the relationship layer: engaging named people on purpose and measuring who engages back. Chapter 5 covers your own posts — the seven series and how they draw from JobOps, learn-ops, and the lifelog without ever writing your opinions for you. Chapter 6 is how the whole thing learns from what you actually do. Chapter 7 is the four rules that never bend, and the one honest chapter about what's still unproven.
