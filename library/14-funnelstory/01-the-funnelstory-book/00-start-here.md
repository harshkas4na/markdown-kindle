# Start here

You do a lot of work at FunnelStory with Claude's help. Some of it you understand. Some of it you just run, and it works, and you move on. This book is for the second kind. It is the thing you read on the train, or in bed, or while the tea is cooling, until the pieces join up and you can say any of it out loud to Arun, Aadarsh or a customer.

It is not a manual. A manual tells you which button to press. This book tells you why the button exists, who asked for it, what went wrong the last time, and what it means for the next call you are on.

> [!NOTE] Client-confidential
> This book names customers, people and internal designs. It lives only inside this reader, in a folder that git ignores. Do not copy it into a public repo, a public note, a post, or a chat with someone outside the company. The NDA covers all of it.

## How the book is built

Six parts, from wide to narrow.

1. **The company.** What FunnelStory sells, who buys it, and the one big idea behind it. Three short chapters.
2. **How it works.** The machine under the hood: how data comes in, becomes tags, becomes a score, becomes a warning, becomes a memory. Seven chapters.
3. **Agents and your craft.** The part you actually build: flow agents, their limits, the draft gate, how a customer is configured, and how you tell whether anyone used what you built. Seven chapters.
4. **The customers.** One story per account. GreyOrange gets three chapters because it is yours.
5. **People and habits.** Who is who, how each founder hears a number, and what "done" means in this job.
6. **The back of the book.** A plain-English glossary, a map from your own work folders to the chapters that explain them, every check question in one place, and the list of images still to collect.

Every chapter has the same shape, so your brain learns where to look:

- **A story first.** Who wanted what, why, and what happened.
- **How it works.** The mechanism in plain words.
- **Where you see it in your week.** The ticket, the customer or the flow where this idea shows up.
- **Three check questions.** Answer them out loud. If you can, you own the chapter. If you cannot, that is a study lab topic.

## How to read it

- One chapter at a time. Ten minutes each, no more.
- Do not try to memorise names. Names are in the people chapter and the glossary when you need them.
- After a chapter, say the three-line version out loud, as if Arun asked. That is the real test. The book is only a good book if you can do that.
- Skip around when a customer call is coming. Part 4 stands alone once you know the words from Parts 1 to 3.

## How sure is each fact

The company changes fast, and a lot of what is known came from calls, Slack and code, not from a briefing. So facts carry one of three marks where it matters:

- **Checked** means someone read it from the live system or the source code.
- **From notes** means it came from the repo record and was not re-checked this week.
- **Heard** means it was said on a call and never confirmed.

Customer numbers in this book are the numbers that were true when they were recorded. Re-check any number before you say it to a customer. That habit is part of the job, and chapter 5.3 explains why.

## What this book is not

It is not the study lab. The lab tests you and records the result. The book prepares you for the lab, and gives you somewhere to read when you do not want to be tested.

It is not the record. The record is the company folder in work-ops: decisions, people, open questions. The book summarises the record into a story. When the book and the record disagree, the record wins, and the book should be fixed.

## The five sentences to carry

If you read nothing else, carry these.

1. FunnelStory helps customer success teams see a lost renewal months before it happens, and then helps them act.
2. The product is built on one bet: the early warning signals live in conversations, and a warehouse cannot see conversations.
3. Your job is to take a vague customer ask, build the missing tenth of the answer by hand, and make sure someone actually uses it.
4. Nothing is switched on, sent or written in a customer workspace without your explicit word. A draft stays a draft.
5. Work is not value. An agent that runs is not an agent that helped.

Turn the page.
